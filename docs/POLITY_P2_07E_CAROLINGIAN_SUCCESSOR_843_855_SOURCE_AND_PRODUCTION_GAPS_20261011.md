## Independent historical cross-check: 843 and 855 are NOT interchangeable

- [Larousse, Traité de Verdun (août 843)](https://www.larousse.fr/encyclopedie/divers/Verdun/148594): postwar territorial partitions allocated western Francia to Charles the Bald, eastern Francia to Louis the German, and a middle realm with the imperial title to Lothair I. These are territorial and operational partitions, not mere orthographic changes to one court.
- [Britannica Carolingian and post-Carolingian Italy](https://www.britannica.com/place/Italy/Carolingian-and-post-Carolingian-Italy-774-962): 843 settlement left West Francia, East Francia and Italy as principal successor regimes; court authority and hereditary imperial status were not simply one unchanged polity after partition.
- [University of Lorraine regional historical history](https://rgh.univ-lorraine.fr/articles/index.html): after Lothair I's death, the **855 Treaty of Prüm** subdivided the middle realm, and the northern portion went to **Lothair II** as Lotharingia; later 869/870 succession and partitions further changed its extent. Therefore the existing Lotharingia 855–869 Activity is *not equivalent* to the WHOLE Middle Francia 843–855.
- [Oxford Academic, Laury Sarti, Imperium in the Carolingian World (2024)](https://academic.oup.com/book/56468/chapter-abstract/448564599): Carolingian emperorship and the underlying Frankish kingship are historically interconnected yet have distinct legal-ideological meanings; do not create imaginary imperial sovereignty breaks just because one Person held two titles.
