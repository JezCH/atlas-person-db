## 2. Primary institutional markers and sovereignty distinction

### March–December 1897: revolutionary election, constitution, exile

The **NHCP official Tejeros Convention historical marker** confirms the meeting March 22, 1897 voted for a central Revolutionary Government and elected Emilio Aguinaldo as President (also Mariano Trías as vice president, cabinet roles); Andres Bonifacio openly challenged its outcome, so **not a universally consolidated separate Philippine sovereign nation-state**. DILG official departmental history traces March 22 cabinet formation and April 17 Naic appointments before November Biak-na-Bato constitution.

The **NHCP Aguinaldo biography marker** provides the **distinct 2 November 1897 commencement of his Republic of Biak-na-Bato presidency**, even where institutional records say the new constitutional text was established or ratified on **1 November**. This is **event-specific dating**, not licence to rewrite existing exact-day fields from a different event. The Presidency of the Republic of Biak-na-Bato ends in Production on **27 December** as Aguinaldo left for self-imposed **Hong Kong exile** following the Dec 14–15 pact. That creates a meaningful **absence of effective domestic government** between the 1897 and 1898 phases.

The Tejeros revolutionary political authority and short-lived Biak-na-Bato constitution thus may be interpreted as linked revolutionary leadership, but their distinct **operational and constitutional stages** justify separate existing Polity UUIDs under the current ATLAS stage-Polity model. There is no invented sovereign extinction at midnight on 1 November.

### May 1898–January 1899: renewed war and constitutional transformation

The NHCP Aguinaldo marker dates his return to the Philippines **19 May 1898**, creation of a **Dictatorial Government 24 May**, Declaration of Independence **12 June**, and replacement by a **Revolutionary Government 23 June 1898**. The existing `dictator` vs `President` role distinction is authentic; the common June 23 1898 calendar-day boundary marks constitutional replacement, **not concurrent offices under two independent sovereign polities**. Malolos Congress assembled 15 September 1898 to formulate constitutional institutions.

The **Philippine Official Gazette** records the Proclamation recognizing the effectiveness/inauguration of the First Philippine Republic on **23 January 1899**. Thus the preceding Revolutionary Government Activity terminates the same calendar day the new republic's presidency commences. Distinguish the **1898 Declaration of Independence** from **1899 constitutional republican inauguration**; they are not equivalent milestones.

Aguinaldo's First Republic Activity ends on his **capture 23 March 1901**, as corroborated by the NHCP marker and National Library. This is an **effective Aguinaldo presidency boundary**, NOT proof that every revolutionary army, government-in-exile or regional autonomy project terminated that day; resistance continued.
