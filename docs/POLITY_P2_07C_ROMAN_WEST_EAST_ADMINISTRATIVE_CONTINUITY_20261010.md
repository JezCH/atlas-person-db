## 2. Independent scholarly source cross-check — crucial historical correction

- [Adrastos Omissi, *Emperors and Usurpers in the Later Roman Empire*, Oxford 2018, Typographical Note](https://academic.oup.com/book/10597/chapter-abstract/158556022): explains that capitalized terms Western/Eastern Roman Empire are useful shorthand **without formalized official east/west state separation**; emperors allocated responsibility along regional lines.
- [*Contested Monarchy: Integrating the Roman Empire in the Fourth Century AD*, Oxford, succession analysis](https://academic.oup.com/book/57638/chapter/478445349): after 395, **Imperium Romanum remained formally united**; the two imperial courts crystallized distinct practical areas of jurisdiction during ensuing decades, amid dynastic and political competition.
- [*The Cambridge Ancient History*, The dynasty of Theodosius](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-ancient-history/dynasty-of-theodosius/13877E996427028C87E955CCD1948F9C): in 395 the empire's two parts were **still constitutionally undivided**, notwithstanding separate courts and political rivalry.
- [Peter Salway, *A History of Roman Britain*, Chapter 16, Oxford 2001](https://academic.oup.com/book/47282/chapter-abstract/422393315): calling 395 an absolute division is an overly simple model; crisis in the western administration accumulated progressively under Honorius, and the Western Roman world fragmented over time.
- [*Oxford Handbook of Byzantine Studies*, Political-Historical Survey c. 250–518](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/29470/chapter-abstract/247163090): presents the customary 395 dynastic administrative partition and the failure of the western court after Romulus Augustulus in 476 while the eastern Roman institutional tradition continued.
- [Oxford Classical Dictionary, Honorius, Roman emperor](https://academic.oup.com/edited-volume/61673/chapter-abstract/548934218): original source reference already included in both exact public Honorius Activities, not replaced in this no-write case.

**Judgment:** Retain three currently distinct **catalog-operational** UUIDs and their existing Person bindings, but explicitly model the former united Roman imperial polity as continuous in constitutional/ideological terms into the east/west courts. The Eastern/Byzantine label is a later historiographic convenience, not proof eastern Romans ceased to be Roman. Neither an original Roman identity's activity ending at 395 nor the existence of a western/eastern Polity UUID demonstrates a new independent sovereign origin at that instant. Any future governance/parent link representation requires a separate actual existing-relations audit and source-backed authoring plan, not fabricated cross-links.
