## 1. Historical decision — preserve dynasty continuity without erasing an operational rupture

The Northern Song (960–1127) to Southern Song (1127–1279) transition resulted from the Jin/Jurchen invasion, the 1127 fall of Kaifeng and loss of North China, with Zhao Gou / Emperor Gaozong restoring the Song court in the south. It was neither a fresh unrelated new dynasty nor a routine spelling change of an otherwise unaltered administrative center. The actual sovereign realm / operated court/defense jurisdiction contracted and institutional structures changed radically. Both strong political continuity and deep operational/territorial break must be represented.

Sources establishing those separate elements:
- [Tao Jing-shen, *The Move to the South and the Reign of Kao-tsung (1127–1162)*, Cambridge History of China (online 2010)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/cambridge-history-of-china/move-to-the-south-and-the-reign-of-kaotsung-11271162/E39FFED3578CF0BA1B97563FDB46DB1B): Gaozong was the son of Huizong and brother of Qinzong, and **restored** Song dynastic government south of lost capital; Southern Song spans 1127–1279.
- [Metropolitan Museum chronology, China 1000–1400](https://www.metmuseum.org/zh/toah/ht/07/eac): one Song ruling tradition 960–1279 divided into Northern 960–1127, Southern 1127–1279; northern capitals captured by the Jurchen Jin, southern court reconstituted.
- [Journal of Chinese History, *Military Institutions as a Defining Feature of the Song Dynasty* (2017)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-chinese-history/article/military-institutions-as-a-defining-feature-of-the-song-dynasty/D020A447BD8666C3304D7A315CB65DFD): major 1127 political/defense institution shift rather than merely a convention in language.
- [Yang Y., *Rectifying Titles: Regnal Names and the Political Processes of the Northern–Southern Song Transition*, CUHK research record, peer-reviewed 2025](https://research.cuhk.edu.hk/en/publications/%E6%8F%AD%E4%B8%95%E8%99%9F%E4%BB%A5%E6%AD%A3%E5%90%8D%E5%B0%8A%E8%99%9F%E8%88%87%E5%85%A9%E5%AE%8B%E4%B9%8B%E4%BA%A4%E6%94%BF%E6%B2%BB%E9%81%8E%E7%A8%8B/): Gaozong invoked the rituals of imperial legitimation during chaotic 1126–1130 transition, reinforcing continuity in dynastic authority.
- [Academia Sinica, *Lin'an and the State Sacrificial Rituals of Southern Song China* (2017)](https://www1.ihp.sinica.edu.tw/en/Publications/Bulletin/1038/Article/1989): the southern state's capital/court restoration was multi-stage; **Lin'an de facto 1138**, not an imagined simultaneous 1127 transfer directly to Hangzhou. Keep 1127 dynasty-period labels distinct from the later formal capital date.

**Bounded decision:** keep **Northern Song** and **Southern Song** as separate normalized operated political-entity representations, *but preserve their shared Song-dynastic lineage*, without any automatic merge, new sovereign founder, deletion or same-year fictitious calendar precision.
